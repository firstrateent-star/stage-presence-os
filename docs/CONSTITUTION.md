# Stage Presence OS Constitution

## Durable principles
1. Stage Presence creates reliable customer outcomes, not merely equipment rentals.
2. One Engagement persists through opportunity, commitment, execution, and closeout.
3. Customer request and actual outcome/requirement remain distinct.
4. Unknown is legitimate data.
5. Important facts need provenance, certainty, and time context.
6. AI inference is not automatically verified business truth.
7. Every active Engagement has a next movement, or is intentionally waiting/blocked.
8. Commercial enthusiasm and operational commitment are different states.
9. Every commitment consumes capacity and creates opportunity cost.
10. Accepted scope should become operational scope without re-entry.
11. Routine continuity belongs to systems; important judgment belongs to people.
12. History is append-oriented; archive rather than silently erase.
13. Reliability is part of the product.
14. Revenue is not the same as value; cash timing differs from profit.
15. Capability does not have to be owned to be sellable.
16. Capital should follow evidence.
17. Growth that proportionally increases founder dependence is weak scaling.
18. Every meaningful recurring process should eventually have an evidence loop.
19. The UI must remain simpler than the business model underneath it.
20. A system that the team does not adopt is not a functioning system.

## Strategic hypotheses — to test, not hard-code
- Stage Presence's strongest center is LED / large-format visual production and integration.
- Audio, lighting, staging, content, and related services are strongest as complementary adjacencies unless data proves otherwise.
- Event production + long-term deployment + installation/service may be healthier than event-only revenue.
- Recurring relationships are more strategically valuable than raw lead count.
- Reducing unnecessary Greg-dependence will unlock disproportionate capacity.
- Some current revenue may be weak once complexity and constrained capacity are considered.
- Existing physical assets may have underutilized or poorly measured economic potential.
