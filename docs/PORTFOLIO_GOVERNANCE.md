# Stage Presence — Portfolio Governance

**Status:** Strategic operating design; economic conclusions remain hypotheses until direct-cost and closeout evidence improves  
**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Purpose:** Help Stage Presence decide not only whether it can perform an Engagement, but whether it should deliberately seek more work like it.

---

## 1. Core principle

Stage Presence has constrained physical assets, skilled labor, working capital and founder attention. Therefore **opportunity quality matters more than raw lead volume**.

Do not optimize the company for:
- number of leads;
- number of jobs;
- revenue alone;
- equipment utilization alone.

The desired portfolio should create sustainable contribution, relationship compounding and productive capacity use without proportional growth in Greg-dependent complexity.

---

## 2. Do not create one opaque opportunity score

Future decision support should expose a profile rather than hide judgment inside one number.

Useful dimensions include:

### Strategic fit
Does the opportunity strengthen Stage Presence's LED/large-format visual center or a deliberately chosen growth engine?

### Relationship leverage
Is this an isolated transaction or part of a recurring planner, venue, university, corporate, hospitality, public-event or partner relationship?

### Economic quality
What contribution is expected after directly caused labor, subcontracting, transport, travel and other engagement-specific costs?

### Capacity efficiency
How much scarce trailer/panel/crew/founder capacity does the Engagement consume relative to contribution?

### Repeatability
Can prior solution archetypes, venue knowledge and learned workflow reduce future sales/design/delivery effort?

### Complexity / risk
How many technical categories, vendors, unknowns, liability points, venue constraints and scope-change opportunities exist?

### Founder dependence
How much Greg-specific sales, technical, negotiation or execution effort is required?

### Revenue duration / strategic option value
Does the Engagement create day/week/month/permanent/service revenue or open a meaningful future pathway?

These dimensions should remain visible separately. A single aggregate score may be added only if evidence later proves it useful and explainable.

## 4. Working portfolio lanes

These are strategic categories for decision-making, not mandatory database statuses.

### Lane A — Core repeatable visual deployment
Examples may include mobile LED trailers, modular walls and repeated visual deployments.

Desired characteristics:
- strong strategic fit;
- repeatable solution pattern;
- controllable logistics;
- clear capacity economics;
- low unnecessary Greg dependence;
- recurring relationship potential.

This is the strongest current candidate for aggressive standardization and scaling.

### Lane B — Strategic full-production / adjacency work
Multi-category production where audio, lighting, staging, power, content or other services strengthen an important visual/customer solution.

Pursue selectively when:
- contribution supports complexity;
- relationship value is meaningful;
- attachments improve win/value/reliability;
- Stage Presence has or can source qualified execution capacity.

Avoid becoming a generic full-service AV company merely because every category can be supplied.

### Lane C — Long-duration / integration growth
Long-term deployment, permanent installation and eventual service/support.

Strategic attraction:
- different revenue duration;
- potential recurring service;
- installed-base relationship compounding;
- possibly less dependence on one-day event sales.

But economics/risk differ materially:
- working capital;
- procurement;
- warranty/support;
- install labor;
- project-management risk;
- longer sales cycle.

Treat as a distinct operating/economic species, not an event job with a longer date range.

### Lane D — Opportunistic sales / commodity work
Equipment sales, simple rentals or low-complexity transactions may be useful when they produce contribution, relationship value, asset rotation or low-friction utilization.

Do not let low-value commodity demand consume scarce strategic capacity merely because it is easy to quote.

---

## 5. Relationship concentration is both asset and risk

The current import shows one recurring contact/program node associated with a very large share of known signed value.

Correct response:
- protect and deepen valuable relationships;
- learn how to make them increasingly reliable/profitable;
- deliberately create additional high-quality recurring nodes;
- do not mistake repeated components of one program for portfolio diversification.

The objective is not to reduce a strong account. It is to build more strong accounts while managing concentration exposure.

---

## 6. Growth should be selective

A useful future qualification question is:

> If Stage Presence could clone this Engagement ten times, would the company become better or worse?

If cloning it would produce:
- strong contribution;
- predictable operations;
- repeat relationships;
- good capacity use;
- low avoidable founder dependence;
then it is a candidate growth pattern.

If cloning it would produce:
- constant custom engineering;
- weak margins;
- severe asset conflicts;
- high subcontract risk;
- customer/payment friction;
- Greg as permanent PM;
then growth may amplify fragility rather than value.

---

## 7. Capital allocation follows portfolio evidence

Buy equipment when evidence shows:
- repeated profitable demand;
- material subcontract spend that ownership can improve;
- lost opportunities due to scarcity;
- strategic differentiation;
- attractive utilization/contribution potential;
- manageable maintenance/transport/storage implications.

Do not buy because:
- a product is exciting;
- one customer asked once;
- utilization alone looks high without contribution;
- a spreadsheet shows a theoretical rental rate.

---

## 8. Desired future portfolio view

The OS should eventually answer:
- Which Engagement types create the most contribution?
- Which relationships create recurring lifetime contribution?
- Which solution archetypes are easiest to repeat reliably?
- Which resources are truly scarce and economically productive?
- Which jobs generate outsized Greg dependence?
- Which venues/planners/accounts reduce acquisition/delivery friction over time?
- Which adjacencies raise value versus merely adding complexity?
- Which install/service opportunities create durable recurring revenue?
- Where is concentration risk growing?

---

## 9. Near-term evidence required

Before portfolio automation, capture:
- quote/contract/final collected value;
- direct labor cost/hours;
- subcontract cost;
- transport/travel cost;
- actual resource requirement windows;
- scope/change-order leakage;
- actual operational variance;
- recurrence/referral evidence;
- Greg-dependent effort where practical.

The lightweight closeout loop is the primary mechanism for acquiring much of this evidence with low additional administration.

---

## 10. Working portfolio north star

> Grow recurring, differentiated, high-contribution work that uses Stage Presence's constrained capability efficiently and becomes easier to deliver with experience—while preventing complexity, concentration and founder dependence from scaling at the same rate as revenue.
