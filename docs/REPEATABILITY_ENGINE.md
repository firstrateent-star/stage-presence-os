# Stage Presence — Repeatability Engine

**Status:** Working operating design informed by the 2026-09-09 Goodshuffle import  
**Purpose:** Turn completed work into reusable business intelligence so future Engagements become faster, safer and more profitable without forcing rigid public packages.

---

## 1. Core principle

Stage Presence should not standardize customer outcomes too early. It should standardize the **reusable internal knowledge** behind recurring solutions.

A delivered Engagement should strengthen four things:

1. **Solution archetype** — what kind of solution worked and what it usually contains;
2. **Venue memory** — what is true about the physical environment;
3. **Operating timing** — how long preparation, possession, setup, event, strike and return actually took;
4. **Economics** — what revenue, direct cost, labor, subcontracting and constrained capacity the outcome actually consumed.

Then the next similar Engagement starts from accumulated evidence rather than Greg reconstructing the job from memory.

## 3. Solution archetype is not a public package

An archetype is an internal starting point, not a promise to the market.

A future archetype may preserve:
- customer outcome/use case;
- common requirements;
- typical configuration;
- optional attachments/adjacencies;
- common unknowns to clarify;
- typical possession/setup/return window;
- usual crew roles;
- venue prerequisites;
- recurring risk checks;
- pricing history/band;
- direct-cost history;
- actual contribution history;
- common exceptions;
- source Engagements that earned the pattern.

The system should create a fresh Engagement and adapt the archetype to reality. It should never silently assert that the template is correct because it matched a label.

## 4. Venue memory

Venue knowledge should become durable company knowledge rather than disappearing into individual job notes.

Current recurrence evidence includes:
- Polk Place — 7 Engagements;
- 1750 Signal Point Rd — 2;
- Riverfront Park — 2;
- The Refinery Charleston — 2.

Useful venue memory can eventually include:
- load-in route and timing;
- parking/truck/trailer access;
- power source/location/capacity;
- display placement and viewing constraints;
- rigging restrictions;
- network/internet reality;
- weather exposure;
- noise/curfew rules;
- venue contacts;
- preferred setup photos / diagrams;
- prior incidents/problems;
- actual setup/strike duration;
- resource substitutions that worked;
- documents and source evidence.

Do not create formal venue-account relationships merely from matching text. Promote venue entities/relationships only when evidence supports identity.

## 5. Learning closeout

A small closeout loop should be promoted earlier than a giant operations module because it creates the evidence required for later pricing, capacity, training and automation.

Target human burden: approximately two minutes for routine jobs, with deeper review only for exceptions.

A useful closeout should capture or infer:
- what resources were actually used versus planned;
- actual possession/setup/event/strike/return windows;
- who worked and approximate labor time where available;
- subcontracted/sourced capacity actually used;
- material scope changes/change orders;
- operational issue/incident/near miss;
- venue learning;
- actual final financial facts when available;
- whether Greg was materially required and why;
- what should change next time;
- likely recurrence / next date / follow-up opportunity.

Most of this should ultimately be prefilled from system events and only ask humans for missing exception knowledge.

---

## 6. Why closeout moves earlier

Without actual-delivery feedback:
- capacity windows remain inferred;
- pricing stays rate-card driven;
- contribution stays unknowable;
- crew/time estimates do not improve;
- venue knowledge remains in people;
- archetypes become guesses;
- annual repeats still require reconstruction.

A lightweight closeout turns every Engagement into training data for the business itself.

---

## 7. Repeatability flywheel

Customer demand
→ candidate archetype / prior relationship / venue memory
→ faster solution design
→ Commitment Ladder / capacity review
→ reliable delivery
→ lightweight closeout
→ actual configuration / timing / cost / venue / relationship learning
→ stronger archetype and better future decisions
→ next Engagement.

The business should become easier to run as its experience grows.

---

## 8. Strategic implications

### Standardize the repeatable visual engine
The current sample supports making common LED deployments increasingly routine, well-understood and low-Greg-dependence.

### Keep complex production exception-driven
Full multi-category productions should reuse components and knowledge but should not be falsely forced into a standard package while evidence remains heterogeneous.

### Measure archetypes economically later
Do not rank archetypes by revenue alone. Eventually compare contribution, constrained capacity consumption, operational variance, recurrence and Greg-dependent effort.

### Learn before buying
Repeated sourcing pressure / lost demand / high-contribution archetypes should inform asset purchases. Equipment enthusiasm should not.

---

## 9. What not to build yet

Do not create a large archetype database or force every current Goodshuffle job into a named template.

Do not infer venue facts from project names.

Do not create customer-facing packages just because internal configurations repeat.

Do not ask field teams to complete long post-event forms.

First prove that a tiny closeout can capture useful actuals with low friction.

---

## 10. Working north star

> Every completed Engagement should leave Stage Presence with more reusable knowledge than it had before the Engagement began.

If future work of the same class is not easier to price, prepare, staff, deliver or sell because of prior experience, institutional knowledge is still leaking out of the company.
