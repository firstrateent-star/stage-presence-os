# Flower Recenter — Stage Presence Business Model × Stage Presence OS

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Status:** Current strategic recenter after Shared Reality, Goodshuffle translation, Business Command, Capacity Truth, Commitment Ladder and Learning Closeout activation.

## New Flower center

> **DECISION-GRADE FLOW**
>
> Stage Presence OS should progressively transform partial business reality into the minimum trustworthy evidence required for the next consequential decision, route only material exceptions to the right human, and allow routine work to advance without reconstructing the whole Engagement in Greg's head.

Alternate plain-language formulation:

> **What decision is next, what must be true to make it responsibly, and who can resolve the exception?**

This center is intentionally broader than quoting, scheduling, inventory, CRM, or operations. It is the membrane connecting Stage Presence's business model to the operating system already built.

---

## 1. Why the center changed

The earlier system problem was lack of shared reality. That justified the initial Shared Reality kernel.

The current system now has:
- one persistent Engagement;
- customer/Party links;
- dates including date-only truth;
- facts, unknowns and conflicts;
- resource configurations;
- capacity requirement windows and pressure signals;
- multidimensional state;
- source provenance;
- business command queues;
- relationship recurrence signals;
- commitment-ladder design;
- learning closeouts and review signals.

The current constraint is therefore no longer simply **remember the business**.

The current constraint is:

> **turn remembered reality into responsible movement without reintroducing founder-dependent reconstruction.**

If the OS only accumulates accurate records but every meaningful step still ends with "Greg, review this," the system has digitized awareness without solving the operating constraint.

## 2. Business-model comparison

### Stage Presence business model says

Stage Presence creates value by converting:
- customer intent;
- relationships/trust;
- physical capability;
- technical knowledge;
- skilled people;
- logistics/time;
- financial capital;
- founder judgment;

into a reliable temporary or permanent customer outcome.

The durable business loop is:

**Demand → Understanding → Solution → Feasibility → Commitment → Capacity → Preparation → Delivery → Closeout → Learning → Relationship / recurrence.**

### Stage Presence OS currently does well

The OS is increasingly strong at:
- sensing/capturing reality;
- preserving evidence and uncertainty;
- maintaining one shared Engagement;
- surfacing demand and delivery attention;
- detecting provisional capacity pressure;
- identifying recurring relationship nodes;
- distinguishing configuration from reservation truth;
- preserving learning after outcomes.

### Stage Presence OS currently does weakly

The OS does not yet derive, in a sufficiently actionable way:
- the next business decision for each Engagement;
- which facts are decision-critical versus merely incomplete;
- whether an Engagement is sufficiently evidenced for Quote Ready, Commit Ready, Reserve Ready or Execute Ready;
- which exception can be resolved by system/Nancy/Sean/operations versus Greg;
- commercial-value evidence for current proposals;
- direct-cost evidence or contribution expectations;
- deposit/payment truth;
- Engagement-specific sourcing truth;
- verified possession/setup/return windows;
- crew/transport execution capacity.

This is the largest current gap between the business model and the OS.

## 4. The new atomic loop

The next operating loop should be:

**ENGAGEMENT REALITY**  
→ **NEXT DECISION**  
→ **DECISION-CRITICAL TRUTHS**  
→ **EXCEPTIONS / MISSING EVIDENCE**  
→ **RESOLUTION OWNER**  
→ **DECISION**  
→ **STATE / COMMITMENT PROPAGATION**  
→ **NEXT DECISION**

This loop repeats throughout one persistent Engagement.

The purpose is not to create more approvals. It is to make routine business move faster by identifying precisely when no additional approval is needed.

## 5. Decision ladder as living operating logic

The existing Commitment Ladder becomes the first implementation of Decision-Grade Flow.

### A. Quote Ready

Question:
> Do we understand enough to propose a legitimate solution and price without misleading the customer or Stage Presence?

Decision-critical truth may include:
- identifiable customer/contact;
- event/project timing sufficient for commercial relevance;
- literal customer request / desired outcome understood enough;
- plausible solution/configuration or explicit assumption;
- material technical unknowns visible;
- capacity pressure visible when relevant;
- pricing evidence or approved pricing path;
- required commercial exception identified.

Quote Ready does not require reservation truth.

### B. Commit Ready

Question:
> Can Stage Presence responsibly accept the customer's yes/signature under the stated assumptions?

Decision-critical truth may include:
- material scope understood;
- major technical feasibility resolved or explicitly conditioned;
- commercial terms/value known;
- known capacity pressure reviewed;
- major sourcing dependency identified;
- unacceptable economic/risk exceptions surfaced;
- decision authority identified.

Commit Ready does not itself mean capacity is reserved.

### C. Reserve Ready

Question:
> Do we have enough evidence to intentionally protect scarce capacity for this Engagement?

Decision-critical truth may include:
- commercial commitment evidence;
- deposit/payment evidence according to policy or explicit exception;
- resource requirement windows strong enough to reserve;
- sourcing model determined;
- actual capacity/quantity truth sufficient for affected scarce resources;
- conflicting reservations resolved;
- hold/reservation authority established.

### D. Execute Ready

Question:
> Can operations deliver the promised outcome reliably?

Decision-critical truth may include:
- operational times/location/access;
- final resource configuration;
- sourcing confirmed;
- crew/technical ownership;
- transport/load-in/load-out;
- content/playback inputs;
- power/network/rigging constraints;
- customer/venue contacts;
- material change orders or unresolved issues;
- handoff ownership.

## 6. The key design principle: relevance beats completeness

Decision-Grade Flow must not turn the OS into a form-completion machine.

The system should not ask:
> Which database fields are blank?

It should ask:
> Which unknown can change the decision we are trying to make now?

Examples:
- unknown cable quantity may be irrelevant to Quote Ready;
- unknown 17×10 trailer possession window may be critical to Commit Ready;
- missing customer's secondary phone number may be irrelevant;
- missing deposit evidence may be decisive for Reserve Ready;
- missing venue load-in path may be irrelevant to quoting but decisive for Execute Ready.

This extends the existing **decision leverage** principle from inventory verification to the whole business.

## 7. Exception-driven authority

Decision-Grade Flow should turn the existing authority architecture into routing logic.

### System / deterministic logic
Handle:
- state propagation;
- date arithmetic;
- known rule checks;
- recurrence detection;
- capacity overlap math;
- stale follow-up resurfacing;
- standard price calculations once governed;
- routine reminders / evidence requests.

### AI / interpretation layer
Eventually support:
- extracting candidate facts from photos/voice/email/text;
- identifying likely missing decision-critical information;
- matching similar jobs/archetypes;
- drafting customer clarification;
- summarizing exceptions;
- recommending next moves.

AI does not silently convert inference into verified truth.

### Nancy / commercial administration
Likely ownership:
- contract/signature evidence;
- deposit/invoice/collection continuity;
- administrative commercial completeness;
- approved routine commercial propagation.

### Sean / commercial + systems + operating coordination
Likely ownership:
- ordinary sales movement;
- structured qualification;
- system/data continuity;
- routine opportunity progression;
- process exceptions that do not require founder authority.

### Operations / technical owner
Likely ownership:
- requirement-window verification;
- sourcing feasibility;
- crew/transport/readiness;
- technical operational exceptions.

### Greg
Reserve for:
- relationship-sensitive decisions;
- significant negotiations;
- material pricing exceptions;
- scarce-capacity tradeoffs;
- unusual technical design;
- capital exposure;
- liability/reputation risk;
- strategic partnerships/opportunities.

The desired outcome is not fewer decisions. It is fewer decisions that unnecessarily require Greg.

## 8. Relationship to the Business Command Layer

Today should increasingly become a queue of **decisions/exceptions**, not Engagement statuses.

Current sections already move toward this:
- Protect Delivery;
- Convert Demand;
- Capacity Pressure;
- Verify Capacity Truth;
- Relationships;
- Unresolved Truth;
- Learn / Resolve.

The next evolution should answer more explicitly:

**What decision is next?**  
**What is preventing safe movement?**  
**Who owns resolving it?**  
**When must it be resolved?**

For example, instead of:

`Review operational readiness`

prefer a derived reason such as:

`Execute Ready blocked: 12x7 sourcing unknown; setup/load-in window inferred only.`

Or instead of:

`Review quote status and set follow-up`

prefer:

`Commercial decision: confirm quote value/source and follow up with customer by Sep 11.`

The OS should route the reason for attention, not just the record.

---

## 9. Relationship to Capture

Capture remains critical, but its purpose becomes sharper.

The system does not need to extract every possible field from a lead sheet/photo/voice note.

It should extract enough to determine:
1. what Engagement this is;
2. what decision is next;
3. what decision-critical information is already present;
4. what materially missing information needs clarification.

This means the future Engagement Interpreter should optimize for **decision utility**, not maximum OCR/field extraction.

## 10. Relationship to Economics

Decision-Grade Flow reveals why Economics should activate soon but selectively.

The OS does not yet need a perfect accounting model.

It first needs enough economic truth to improve decisions:
- quote/contract value;
- major direct labor estimate;
- major subcontract cost;
- travel/delivery cost when material;
- unusual procurement/capital exposure;
- eventually actual collected amount and actual direct cost.

This supports:
- pricing exception detection;
- contribution guardrails;
- portfolio quality;
- capital allocation;
- post-job plan-vs-actual learning.

The first economic objective is therefore **decision-grade contribution evidence**, not financial-reporting completeness.

QuickBooks remains the accounting system unless evidence earns another boundary.

---

## 11. Relationship to Capacity Truth

Capacity Truth becomes a direct decision input rather than a standalone feature.

The question is not:
> Is the capacity table complete?

It is:
> Is capacity truth strong enough for the decision being made?

Quote Ready may tolerate inferred timing.
Commit Ready may require a pressure review.
Reserve Ready should require stronger resource windows/sourcing/capacity evidence.
Execute Ready requires operationally actionable timing/sourcing.

This allows capacity evidence to mature gradually without blocking legitimate commercial speed.

---

## 12. Relationship to Learning / Repeatability

Learning Closeout now becomes the mechanism that reduces future decision friction.

Actual outcomes should improve:
- archetype confidence;
- requirement-window defaults;
- setup/strike estimates;
- sourcing patterns;
- venue knowledge;
- direct-cost assumptions;
- Greg-dependence expectations;
- recurrence intelligence.

The long-term compounding loop is:

**Decision → Delivery → Actuals → Learning → Better Defaults → Fewer Exceptions → Faster Decision.**

This is the connection between learning and scale.

## 13. Business-model stress test

### If Stage Presence optimizes only sales speed
Risk:
faster low-quality commitments, capacity surprises, margin leakage.

### If Stage Presence optimizes only operational certainty
Risk:
slow quotes, over-administration, lost demand.

### If Stage Presence optimizes only utilization
Risk:
cheap work occupies scarce capacity and crowds out better relationships/opportunities.

### If Stage Presence optimizes only revenue
Risk:
complexity, labor, subcontracting, capital exposure and Greg dependence remain invisible.

### If Stage Presence optimizes only automation
Risk:
software makes confident decisions on weak evidence.

### Decision-Grade Flow balances these
It asks for the minimum additional certainty needed for the next consequential decision, then moves.

## 14. What should *not* become the next center

### Not "CRM completeness"
Customer records matter, but they are supporting truth.

### Not "inventory"
Capacity matters only relative to demand/commitment and timing.

### Not "quote builder"
Quote speed without decision quality can amplify poor commitments.

### Not "warehouse"
Important later for Execute Ready and actuals, but not the largest current business/OS mismatch.

### Not "AI intake"
High leverage, but it improves sensing. The present bottleneck is using reality to move work responsibly.

### Not "profitability dashboard"
Important, but current direct-cost truth is too weak for a trustworthy dashboard.

### Not "automation everywhere"
Automation should follow repeatability and governed authority.

## 15. Next Flower experiments from the new center

These are evidence experiments, not a mandatory feature backlog.

### Experiment 1 — Derive next decision
For every active Engagement, derive the most relevant current decision:
- qualify/clarify;
- quote;
- follow up/convert;
- commit review;
- reserve review;
- execute readiness;
- learn/resolve;
- relationship follow-up.

Evaluate whether this creates a clearer operating queue than raw next-action text.

### Experiment 2 — Derive decision-critical gaps
For each next decision, show at most the few missing/weak truths that could materially change it.

Measure whether generic `review` next actions can be replaced with specific exceptions.

### Experiment 3 — Route ownership
Map each exception to:
- SYSTEM;
- NANCY;
- SEAN;
- OPERATIONS/TECHNICAL;
- GREG.

Do not create new user accounts or permissions merely to run this logical experiment.

### Experiment 4 — Near-term signed work
Use the next real signed Engagements to discover what Execute Ready actually requires in practice.

Do not invent a comprehensive readiness checklist upfront.

### Experiment 5 — Current proposals
Use current/future proposed Engagements to discover the minimum evidence needed for a high-speed Quote Ready / Commit Ready path.

### Experiment 6 — Economic minimum viable truth
Capture only cost/value information that would change a pricing/commitment decision. Avoid accounting duplication.
