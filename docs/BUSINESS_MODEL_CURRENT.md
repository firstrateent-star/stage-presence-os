# Stage Presence — Current Business Model Reference

**Status:** Working reference for Stage Presence and the Stage Presence OS build  
**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Purpose:** Preserve the current scope of the company as understood through the Vlourish/Flower work so future builders do not reduce Stage Presence to a CRM, equipment list, or quoting workflow.

This document deliberately separates **observed/current context**, **strategic thesis**, and **open hypotheses**. It is not a substitute for audited financial records, signed contracts, or a finalized strategic plan.

---

## 1. Company identity

Stage Presence is a Charleston-area live visual production company centered on deployable LED/display capability and supported by audio, lighting, staging, rigging, content, technical labor, logistics, and installation/integration capability.

The strongest current strategic formulation is:

> **Stage Presence is an LED-first live visual production and integration company that converts customer communication and experiential objectives into reliable temporary or permanent production systems.**

This is intentionally broader than “LED rental” and narrower/more differentiated than “generic AV company.” The customer is ultimately buying an outcome: people can see, hear, experience, or interact with what matters, reliably, in the real environment.

### Durable value-creation model

Customer intent → requirements → solution → feasibility → commitment → capacity → preparation → delivery → closeout → learning → relationship / recurrence.

Stage Presence monetizes access to a changing network of capabilities rather than merely selling individual equipment SKUs.

## 3. Current commercial engines

Stage Presence already behaves like several related businesses sharing the same capabilities and relationships.

### A. Live event production

Short-duration engagements where Stage Presence may provide equipment, delivery, setup, operation, labor, project management, and strike.

Representative customer use cases include:
- sports and athletics
- races and recurring public events
- festivals
- hospitality and hotels
- corporate events
- venue activations
- universities
- private events

### B. Equipment / capacity rental

Temporary access to owned or sourced production capacity. Examples include daily/week/month LED deployment and modular display rentals.

The economics are driven heavily by asset utilization, logistics, time occupancy, and contribution per constrained resource day.

### C. Long-term deployment

Weeks or months of deployed equipment. This can improve predictable utilization but creates opportunity cost because scarce equipment is unavailable for short-duration events.

### D. Permanent installation / integration

Stage Presence designs, supplies, installs, commissions, and potentially supports permanent audiovisual/display systems. This is a different operational species with longer sales cycles, procurement/working-capital exposure, installation labor, warranty/service implications, and potentially much larger project values.

### E. Equipment sale

Selected equipment/system sales or used-equipment transactions. This may be opportunistic, integration-linked, or potentially productized in the future, but is not currently treated as the company’s primary identity.

### F. Service / support opportunity

A future/expanding engine around installed systems, maintenance, support, upgrades, and potentially content/system management. This is strategically attractive because an installed base can create recurring demand without requiring a new event sale every time.

## 6. Customer and relationship model

Stage Presence should not think only in individual “customers” or individual jobs. Commercial value can compound through relationship nodes.

Important relationship archetypes include:
- event planners / agencies
- venues / hotels
- recurring annual events
- universities / athletics
- corporate and hospitality accounts
- production partners
- installation customers
- direct one-time customers

The buyer, payer, planner, venue contact, referrer, and end user may all be different entities within one Engagement.

### Relationship principle

A smaller first job from a planner or venue capable of generating many future engagements may be strategically more valuable than a larger isolated transaction.

The system should eventually measure **relationship lifetime contribution**, recurrence, referral paths, payment behavior, and delivery history rather than raw lead count alone.

## 9. Economic model

Stage Presence converts several scarce forms of capital into outcomes:
- financial capital
- physical assets
- skilled human capability
- time/capacity
- relationships/trust
- technical and venue knowledge
- founder attention

### Engagement economics

Core operating economic measure:

**Engagement Revenue − directly caused engagement costs = Engagement Contribution**

Revenue alone is not sufficient. Two jobs with the same revenue may consume radically different labor, travel, subcontract spend, or scarce trailer-days.

A deeper future measure is:

**Contribution per constrained resource unit**

Examples:
- contribution per trailer-day
- contribution per critical crew-hour
- contribution per Greg-dependent hour

### Asset economics

Major assets should eventually reveal:
- acquisition cost
- booking/utilization history
- revenue/contribution supported
- maintenance cost
- conflict/lost-demand history
- useful life and replacement need

Capital purchases should follow observed demand, margin opportunity, subcontract spend, lost opportunities, strategic differentiation, and opportunity cost—not equipment enthusiasm alone.

### Cash model

Profitability and cash timing are separate. Deposits/progress billing should mirror Stage Presence’s risk and working-capital exposure, especially for installations and equipment procurement.

## 10. Growth model

Stage Presence should not optimize merely for more leads or more revenue.

Primary growth levers include:
- improve conversion of legitimate demand
- shorten opportunity → commitment time
- increase average engagement value through complete solutions
- improve productive utilization of constrained assets
- deepen recurring relationships
- capture annual event recurrence proactively
- improve pricing and contribution discipline
- reduce operational waste
- reduce unnecessary founder dependence
- train additional qualified technical capacity
- add assets only where evidence justifies ownership
- grow profitable long-term deployment and installation/service engines if evidence supports them

### Scaling test

Healthy growth should increase sustainable contribution **without proportionally increasing Greg-dependent complexity, operational fragility, or capital waste**.

## 12. Strategic hypotheses to test

1. LED/large-format visual is the company’s strongest economic and brand center.
2. Audio, lighting, staging, and related services create more value as attachments/adjacencies than as equal standalone strategic centers.
3. Recurring planners, venues, annual events, universities, and corporate relationships are economically more valuable than increasing raw one-off lead count.
4. Greg’s cognitive bandwidth is a major current constraint and systemization can unlock meaningful capacity.
5. Some current revenue is likely less attractive after direct cost, capacity consumption, complexity, and founder time are considered.
6. Long-term deployments and permanent installs/service may diversify revenue duration and improve economic stability.
7. The existing asset base contains underutilized or poorly measured productive potential.
8. Institutional knowledge—customer, venue, technical, pricing, incident, crew, and asset history—can become a meaningful competitive moat if captured and reused.

The operating system should gather evidence capable of confirming or disproving these hypotheses.

## 14. Stage Presence OS relationship to the business

Stage Presence OS exists to improve the company’s conversion of relationships, capabilities, capacity, knowledge, time, and capital into profitable customer outcomes.

Its first responsibility is **Shared Business Reality**—not replacing every existing tool.

The eventual system should help Stage Presence answer:
- What opportunities exist?
- What does the customer actually need?
- What do we know, and how certain are we?
- What capability can satisfy the need?
- Can we responsibly commit it?
- What happens next and who owns it?
- What did we promise?
- What did delivery actually consume?
- Did the Engagement create contribution and cash?
- What did we learn?
- What relationship or recurring demand follows?
- Where should the next unit of Greg attention, labor, equipment, or capital go?

## 16. One-paragraph reference

> **Stage Presence is currently best modeled as an LED-first live visual production and integration company that assembles owned and sourced physical assets, skilled people, technical knowledge, logistics, and customer relationships into reliable temporary or permanent production outcomes. Event production and rental form the primary visible operating engine, with long-term deployments, permanent installations, complementary AV services, selected equipment sales, and future service/support representing additional revenue engines.
